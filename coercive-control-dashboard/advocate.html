Chain of Custody — Evidence Checklist
  • Before handling: photograph evidence in situ, timestamped
  • Record who collected, when, and where each item was found
  • Package in clean container; label with date, item description, collector name
  • Digital evidence (phone, laptop): do not turn on — hand to law enforcement or forensic professional
  • Screenshot digital messages: include sender, timestamp, and full thread context
  • Export call logs as PDF where possible; note carrier for subpoena purposes
  • Preserve voicemails: do not delete, record to separate device if necessary
  • Log all persons who access or handle evidence from this point forward
  • Store in a location unknown to the subject; encrypted cloud backup recommended
  • Provide your attorney with the log — not the originals — for initial review

Digital Safety Reference

Stalkerware detection, device audit, and law enforcement coordination guide for advocates.

Stalkerware Indicators
  • Battery drains unusually fast; device runs warm at rest
  • Mobile data usage spikes with no explanation
  • Device takes longer than usual to shut down
  • Abuser references private conversations or locations they could not otherwise know
  • New apps appeared without your knowledge
  • Screen lights up with no notification
  • Unusual background noise during calls
  • Abuser has had unsupervised physical access to your device
IMPORTANT

Do not remove stalkerware from a device that will be used in legal proceedings. Removal destroys forensic evidence. Consult law enforcement or the Coalition Against Stalkerware (stopstalkerware.org) first.

Safe Device Checklist
  • Use a device the abuser does not know about (library, shelter, new prepaid)
  • Use private/incognito browsing; clear history after each session
  • Use Signal for encrypted communications (disappearing messages if needed)
  • Create a new email account on a new device, not on a shared network
  • Audit connected accounts: Google, Apple, Amazon — remove shared family access
  • Disable location sharing on all apps; review Google Timeline settings
  • Change passwords on a safe device — not on the monitored device
  • Enable two-factor authentication on all critical accounts with a new number
Law Enforcement Coordination — Advocate Notes
01

Request a copy of the police report immediately. Officers are legally required to provide it within a specific timeframe in most states. Verify the domestic flag was applied.

02

If digital evidence is present (stalkerware, intercepted messages), request referral to a digital forensics unit. Many departments have specialized DV digital units. Document the referral date and officer name.

03

VAWA requires that victims' immigration status not be disclosed by law enforcement and that U-visas are available for cooperative crime victims. Flag this proactively for immigrant survivors before any contact with officers.

04

The Lautenberg Amendment prohibits domestic violence misdemeanants from possessing firearms. If firearms are present, request this be investigated and gun-surrender orders enforced.

05

Maintain your own log of all law enforcement contacts: officer name, badge number, date, outcome. This protects survivors when officers fail to act and is admissible in civil proceedings.
