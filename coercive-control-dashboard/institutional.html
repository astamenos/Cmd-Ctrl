Critical Deadline — OSHA § 11(c)
OSHA's anti-retaliation provision has the strictest filing window of any federal statute on this page. If an employer retaliated against you for reporting a workplace safety violation, you have: 30 days to file from the date of the retaliatory act. Miss this window and you permanently lose your federal OSHA remedy.

Workplace Abuse Checklist

Recognizing the Pattern

Coercive control often follows survivors into the workplace. These behaviors may constitute hostile work environment, stalking, or retaliation under federal and state law.

From an intimate partner
  • Calls, texts, or shows up at your workplace repeatedly despite requests to stop
  • Contacts your employer, supervisor, or coworkers to damage your reputation
  • Threatens to sabotage your employment, report you to HR, or send embarrassing material
  • Monitors your location via device or shared apps during work hours
  • Controls access to transportation, childcare, or finances to prevent you from going to work
  • Causes injuries timed to your work schedule to force absences
  • Uses your workplace contact information gained without consent
From an employer or coworker
  • Disproportionate discipline after you disclosed a personal safety situation
  • Denied leave, accommodation request, or schedule flexibility without explanation
  • HR investigation opened shortly after you raised a concern or complaint
  • Performance review downgraded after return from leave
  • Transferred, reassigned, or isolated after disclosing abuse to a supervisor
  • Coworker or supervisor shares or amplifies personal information about your situation
  • Employer failed to take action after you reported workplace harassment or safety threat
Document Everything

Pattern evidence matters in workplace coercive control claims. Each incident alone may fall below a legal threshold — the pattern across incidents is the claim. Use the template below to log each event with date, time, witnesses, and outcome.
